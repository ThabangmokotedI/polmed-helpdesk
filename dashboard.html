WhatsApp API — Auto-create tickets from messages

When a member WhatsApps your helpdesk number, a ticket is automatically created with status New. This uses Meta's free WhatsApp Cloud API (1 000 free conversations per month).

Part A — Create your Meta Developer App (do this once)
1
Open your browser and go to: developers.facebook.com. Click Log in and use your Facebook account (or create one — it's free).
2
Once logged in, click the big blue My Apps button at the top right, then click Create App.
3
You'll see a list of app types. Click Other, then click Next. On the next screen choose Business, then click Next again.
4
Give your app a name — type POLMED Helpdesk. Leave the email as-is. Click Create App.
5
You'll land on a dashboard. Scroll down until you see a box that says WhatsApp. Click the Set Up button inside that box.
6
Meta gives you a free test phone number automatically. You'll see it on screen. Write it down — this is the number members will WhatsApp.
7
On the left menu, click WhatsApp → Configuration. You'll see a box labelled Webhook. Click Edit.
8
In the Callback URL box, paste your Netlify webhook URL. It looks like: https://YOUR-SITE.netlify.app/.netlify/functions/whatsapp-webhook. (You'll get this URL after deploying to Netlify — see the Netlify setup guide in the README.)
9
In the Verify Token box, type: polmed-wa-2026. This is a secret password that proves the webhook is yours. Click Verify and Save.
10
Still on the Configuration page, scroll down to Webhook fields. Find messages in the list and click the Subscribe button next to it. Click Done.
Part B — Add your credentials to Netlify
11
Go to netlify.com, open your site, click Site configuration → Environment variables → Add a variable.
12
Add WHATSAPP_VERIFY_TOKEN = polmed-wa-2026
13
Add FIREBASE_PROJECT_ID = your Firebase project ID (e.g. polmed-helpdesk)
14
Add FIREBASE_SERVICE_ACCOUNT = the JSON key from Firebase. To get this: go to console.firebase.google.com → your project → Project settings (gear icon) → Service accounts → Generate new private key. A file downloads. Open it, select all the text, and paste it as the value here.
15
Click Deploy site in Netlify to apply the new variables. Done! Test it by sending a WhatsApp message to your test number — a ticket should appear in the system within seconds.

Email-to-Ticket — Auto-create tickets from inbound emails

When someone emails your helpdesk address, a ticket is automatically created. This uses Mailgun's free plan (100 emails/day free — no credit card needed for the sandbox).

Part A — Create a free Mailgun account
1
Go to mailgun.com. Click Sign Up Free. Fill in your name, email and a password. Click Create Account.
2
Mailgun gives you a free sandbox domain. It looks like: sandbox1234abcd.mailgun.org. Click on it in the left menu to open it.
3
On the left menu click Receiving then click Create Route.
4
Under Expression type choose Match Recipient. In the box type: tickets@YOUR-SANDBOX.mailgun.org (use your actual sandbox address).
5
Under Actions, tick the box next to Forward. In the box that appears, paste your Netlify email webhook URL: https://YOUR-SITE.netlify.app/.netlify/functions/email-webhook
6
Click Create Route. Done! Now any email sent to tickets@YOUR-SANDBOX.mailgun.org will automatically create a ticket in this system.
Part B — Add Mailgun key to Netlify
7
In Mailgun, go to Settings → API Keys. Copy the Private API key.
8
In Netlify → Site configuration → Environment variables, add: MAILGUN_SIGNING_KEY = the key you just copied.
9
Redeploy your Netlify site. Test by sending an email to your Mailgun address — a ticket with channel Email should appear within seconds.

Role Management — Supervisor vs Agent

Agents can create, view, and update tickets but . Supervisors can do everything including delete tickets. Roles are set once in the Firebase console.

How to make someone a Supervisor
1
Go to console.firebase.google.com and open your project.
2
In the left menu, click Firestore Database.
3
You will see a list of collections. Click on agents. (If it's not there yet, ask the person to log in first — a document is created automatically when someone logs in.)
4
Click the document for the person you want to promote. You'll see their email and a role field that says agent.
5
Click the pencil ✏ icon next to the role field. Change the value from agent to supervisor. Click Update.
6
Ask that person to sign out and sign back in. They will now see the delete button on tickets and the 🛡 Supervisor badge in the sidebar.
How to add a new agent
7
In the Firebase console, go to Authentication → Users. Click Add user.
8
Enter the agent's email address and a temporary password. Click Add user.
9
Share the email and temporary password with the agent. When they log in for the first time, an agents document is automatically created for them with role agent.
10
To let them reset their own password, go to Authentication → Users → click the three dots next to their name → Send password reset email.

⚠ POPIA Compliance Rules

  • Member numbers only — never store full phone numbers. Use the member's Polmed membership number as the identifier.
  • Restricted access — only authorised helpdesk agents should have login credentials.
  • Retention — records older than 12 months must be anonymised or deleted.
  • No external sharing — never export or share ticket data with any party outside the Polmed helpdesk team.
  • Audit trail — every change is logged with the agent's email and timestamp.

WhatsApp auto-ticket workflow

1
A member messages the WhatsApp helpdesk number with an app issue.
2
During business hours (Mon–Fri, 08:00–17:00 SA time, excluding SA public holidays), a ticket is created with status New and the member gets an interactive list asking them to pick an issue type. Outside business hours, the ticket is still created, but the member instead gets an after-hours message asking them to describe their issue, membership number, ID number, and email so an agent can follow up once back online.
3
If the member picks an issue type from the list, it's filled in on the ticket automatically — always double-check it against what they actually wrote before relying on it.
4
The ticket appears instantly in the system. Click it, review the original message and any conversation so far, and fill in whatever's still missing: Issue Type (if not already set), Description, and Status.
5
Reply directly from the ticket with Send via WhatsApp. You can also attach a file (📎), quote a specific member message before replying (↩), or react to one of their messages with an emoji.
6
While you're typing a reply, other agents viewing the same ticket see an "already replying" notice, so two agents don't cross-reply to the same member.
7
When resolved, update the ticket status to Resolved, fill in the resolution notes, and save.

Media, replies & reactions

1
Photos, videos, voice notes, and documents a member sends are automatically downloaded and stored — nothing depends on WhatsApp's own temporary media links, which expire.
2
When you open a ticket, each attachment loads via a short-lived link generated at that moment — this is intentional, so a link copied into a log or chat elsewhere can't be used to view a member's photo indefinitely.
3
You can attach your own file when replying (📎 Attach file, up to 5MB), quote a specific member message before replying (↩ on that message), or send a quick emoji reaction without typing a reply at all.

Continuation suggestion (merge flow)

1
If a member's last ticket was closed within the past 30 days and they message again, the system does not automatically reopen it — it creates a new ticket as usual, but flags it.
2
Opening that new ticket shows a banner naming the earlier ticket and asking whether this message is a continuation of it.
3
Choosing Yes, merge moves the new message onto the original ticket, reopens it to In Progress, and marks the duplicate as Merged. Choosing No, keep separate just dismisses the banner and the new ticket proceeds normally.

System health monitoring

1
Every webhook run reports its status to the system, shown live as a red banner at the top of the dashboard if something's actually wrong (a config problem or a processing error).
2
A rejected request with a bad or forged signature is not treated as a system problem — that's the security check doing its job, not a fault, so it won't trigger a banner or an alert.
3
On a genuine problem, an alert email goes out automatically (rate-limited to once per 30 minutes, so a run of repeated failures doesn't flood the inbox).

Email-to-ticket workflow

1
A member (or team member) sends an email to the helpdesk email address (set up via Mailgun — see Setup page).
2
The email is automatically converted into a ticket with status New and channel Email. The subject becomes the description.
3
Triage the ticket just like a WhatsApp one: fill in Issue Type, add your notes, update status.
